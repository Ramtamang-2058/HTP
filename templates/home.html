Dillibazar, Kathmandu · Established 1984

The company that taught
the computer to read Nepali.

In 1983, Muni Bahadur Sakya demonstrated the first computer that could read and display Devanagari script. In 1984 he founded High Tech Pioneer — Nepal's first commercial computer hardware company, registered while the country was still the Kingdom of Nepal. We have been building at home ever since.

What we build now

Two robots, one lab.

InMoov humanoid robot

A life-size, 3D-printed humanoid built and programmed in Kathmandu. Whisper speech recognition for Nepali and English, a vision-language model for scene understanding, face and emotion recognition, and a Python master control system with graceful fallbacks — a robot that sees, listens and answers.

Software lead: Ram Kumar Tamang · Research: Subash Sigdel · Hardware: lab team

AI wheeled robot

An autonomous mobile platform on NVIDIA Jetson Nano: real-time object detection, voice commands, visitor recognition and ultrasonic obstacle avoidance — engineered end-to-end in Nepal for Nepali users.

Heritage

We were building computers here before it was obvious.

A hand-soldered microcomputer in 1979. Devanagari on screen in 1983. Nineteen Nepali-language computers for the Agriculture Development Bank in 1985. A computer-card factory in 1995. A speaking Nepali robot in 2005. Each one happened because a small team refused to accept that hardware had to be imported.

That instinct is unchanged. The tools are better now — 3D printers, embedded GPUs, open models — but the method is the same: build it here, make it work, write down what we learn.
